## Official Course Description

Artificial Intelligence Fundamentals introduces students to the
foundations and practical applications of Artificial Intelligence (AI),
including models, tools, and prompt engineering techniques to improve AI
outputs. Students will explore AI tools to improve AI-generated output
and analyze ethical, regulatory, and privacy concerns surrounding AI
usage and content ownership.

* **Credits:** 3.0 lecture credits, 3.0 periods, 3.0 load. Subject
  type: Occupational.
* **Requisites (verbatim):** Prerequisites: None. Basic computer
  literacy (e.g., navigating web-based tools, file management) and
  college-level reading (RDG100 or equivalent) and writing proficiency
  (ENG101 or equivalent) is suggested but not required.
* **Course note (verbatim):** CIS137 will require students to create
  accounts to multiple generative AI tools.
* **District first term:** 2026 Fall. Phoenix College's first offering
  is an open HQ decision and never appears in chapter content
  (schedule neutrality, rubric gate G2).

## MCCCD Official Course Competencies (verbatim)

The parentheses name the outline sections each competency serves.

1. Evaluate AI-generated responses for accuracy. (I-V)
2. Differentiate the major types of Artificial Intelligence (AI). (I, III, IV)
3. Demonstrate effective prompting strategies to improve AI outputs. (II, III, V)
4. Analyze the current state of AI and its impact on society, including strengths and weaknesses. (III-V)
5. Apply ethical guidelines to make responsible decisions when using AI tools. (III-V)
6. Select appropriate AI solutions to address specific problems. (IV, V)

## Elevated Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs)

The district wrote every competency with a measurable verb that already
sits at the Apply, Analyze, or Evaluate level of Revised Bloom's
Taxonomy. The elevated CLOs therefore keep the official wording word
for word and add the level. MLOs in every chapter ladder up to these.

* **CLO I (Evaluate):** Evaluate AI-generated responses for accuracy.
* **CLO II (Analyze):** Differentiate the major types of Artificial
  Intelligence (AI).
* **CLO III (Apply):** Demonstrate effective prompting strategies to
  improve AI outputs.
* **CLO IV (Analyze):** Analyze the current state of AI and its impact
  on society, including strengths and weaknesses.
* **CLO V (Apply):** Apply ethical guidelines to make responsible
  decisions when using AI tools.
* **CLO VI (Evaluate):** Select appropriate AI solutions to address
  specific problems.

**Bloom's reading of the district verbs:** *evaluate* and *select* sit
at Evaluate, *differentiate* and *analyze* at Analyze, *demonstrate* and
*apply* at Apply. The MLO band for this course is Apply through
Evaluate. Remember and Understand appear only as steps inside a chapter
(a first Quick Check question, a first Retrieval Practice item), never
as an MLO tag. No MLO is tagged Create: the final project asks students
to select, apply, and evaluate a solution, which is the ceiling the
competencies set.

## The Certificate CIS137 Opens

CIS137 is the first course of the Foundations of Applied Artificial
Intelligence Certificate of Completion (major 5364N, 9 credits):
CIS137, then CIS144 (prerequisite: a C or better in AIM100 or CIS137),
then CIS218 (prerequisite: a C or better in CIS144). The certificate's
seven learning outcomes, verbatim, each served by all three courses:

1. Apply foundational Artificial Intelligence concepts and tools to solve business problems across domains such as marketing, finance, operations, and customer experience. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)
2. Evaluate appropriate AI technologies (e.g., machine learning, natural language processing, predictive analytics) based on business objectives and data availability. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)
3. Apply AI tools to address business problems. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)
4. Interpret AI-generated outputs for factual accuracy. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)
5. Design solutions with ethical and regulatory considerations when utilizing AI. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)
6. Evaluate case studies to understand the opportunities and limitations of AI in organizations. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)
7. Recommend practical ways AI can support efficiency or innovation in business contexts ethically and responsibly including workforce impact. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)

**Scope boundary for this book.** Agents, analytics pipelines, feature
engineering, modeling, and deployment belong to CIS144 and CIS218. The
CIS137 book points ahead to them (in Chapter 9's trends section and in
the Course Conclusion) without teaching them. The full reading of what
CIS137 sets up for the next two courses is in `docs/mcccd/index.html`.

As of the 2026-10-21 fetch, the certificate page lists Scottsdale
Community College as the initiating college and SC as the only program
availability. Adding Phoenix College is a curriculum step outside this
build. Nothing in the book assumes it has happened.
