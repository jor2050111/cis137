## Official Course Description

Artificial Intelligence Fundamentals introduces students to the
foundations and practical applications of Artificial Intelligence (AI),
including models, tools, and prompt engineering techniques to improve AI
outputs. Students will explore AI tools to improve AI-generated output
and analyze ethical, regulatory, and privacy concerns surrounding AI
usage and content ownership.

**Requisites:** Prerequisites: None. Basic computer literacy (e.g.,
navigating web-based tools, file management) and college-level reading
(RDG100 or equivalent) and writing proficiency (ENG101 or equivalent) is
suggested but not required.

**Course Notes:** CIS137 will require students to create accounts to
multiple generative AI tools.

**Credits:** Lecture 3.0 credits, 3.0 periods, 3.0 load. Subject type:
Occupational. First term: 2026 Fall. MCCCD Governing Board approval
date: December 9, 2025.

## MCCCD Official Course Competencies (verbatim)

1. Evaluate AI-generated responses for accuracy. (I-V)
2. Differentiate the major types of Artificial Intelligence (AI). (I, III, IV)
3. Demonstrate effective prompting strategies to improve AI outputs. (II, III, V)
4. Analyze the current state of AI and its impact on society, including strengths and weaknesses. (III-V)
5. Apply ethical guidelines to make responsible decisions when using AI tools. (III-V)
6. Select appropriate AI solutions to address specific problems. (IV, V)

metrics for student work. Please see individual course syllabi for
additional information, including specific course requirements.*

## Elevated Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs)

MCCCD wrote the six competencies with measurable Revised Bloom's
Taxonomy verbs, so the CLOs keep the official wording word for word and
add only the RBT level. MLOs in every chapter must ladder up to these.
Chapters cite them as CLO 1 through CLO 6, the same numbers MCCCD uses,
so outline sections (Roman) and competencies (Arabic) never collide.

* **CLO 1 (Evaluate):** Evaluate AI-generated responses for accuracy.
* **CLO 2 (Analyze):** Differentiate the major types of Artificial Intelligence (AI).
* **CLO 3 (Apply):** Demonstrate effective prompting strategies to improve AI outputs.
* **CLO 4 (Analyze):** Analyze the current state of AI and its impact on society, including strengths and weaknesses.
* **CLO 5 (Apply):** Apply ethical guidelines to make responsible decisions when using AI tools.
* **CLO 6 (Evaluate):** Select appropriate AI solutions to address specific problems.

**How the RBT levels were assigned:** "Evaluate" and "Analyze" are RBT
level names. "Differentiate" is an Analyze verb in the reference
(`docs/blooms-taxonomy-reference.md`). "Demonstrate" and "Apply" are
Apply verbs. "Select" among competing solutions for a stated problem,
with a justified choice, is an Evaluate task, so CLO 6 is tagged
Evaluate. The course band is therefore Apply through Evaluate, with
Create reserved for the Chapter 12 project. Remember and Understand
appear only as steps inside a chapter, never as an MLO level.

## The Certificate Context

CIS137 is the first of three courses in the Foundations of Applied
Artificial Intelligence Certificate of Completion (CCL), major 5364N,
9 credits, catalog year 2026-2027, effective Fall 2026. The other two
are CIS144 Applied Artificial Intelligence (prerequisite: a grade of C
or better in AIM100 or CIS137) and CIS218 Industry Application for
Artificial Intelligence (prerequisite: a grade of C or better in
CIS144). As of 2026-10-21 the program page lists Scottsdale Community
College as the initiating college and SC as the only availability.

The seven program learning outcomes (verbatim, each served by all
three courses):

1. Apply foundational Artificial Intelligence concepts and tools to solve business problems across domains such as marketing, finance, operations, and customer experience. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)
2. Evaluate appropriate AI technologies (e.g., machine learning, natural language processing, predictive analytics) based on business objectives and data availability. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)
3. Apply AI tools to address business problems. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)
4. Interpret AI-generated outputs for factual accuracy. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)
5. Design solutions with ethical and regulatory considerations when utilizing AI. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)
6. Evaluate case studies to understand the opportunities and limitations of AI in organizations. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)
7. Recommend practical ways AI can support efficiency or innovation in business contexts ethically and responsibly including workforce impact. (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218)

**What stays out of this book:** building agents, analytics pipelines,
data cleaning, feature engineering, modeling, and deployment belong to
CIS144 and CIS218. CIS137 points ahead to them (Chapter 9 and Chapter
12) without teaching them. Full outlines: `docs/mcccd/index.html`.
