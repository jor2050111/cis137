Chapter 6 taught "a revision that changed two things at once" as the failure to avoid. This Fix It changes two lines in the prompt, plus a third the reader cannot see. The Tool line edit is also inert in the run, because both prompts went to the same model. So the output change can be credited only to the ticket text. The symptom itself is real: reply 1 in Prompt 14's log repeats "Mr. Hal Benton's home exercise sheet" and adds "We have your number, 602-555-0131."
Fix: Re-capture Prompts 14 and 15 with ticket 2 already brand-free, and drop the Tool line from both sent prompts. A tool does not need to be told which tool it is, and the template keeps "Tool (from your Tool Survey) and date" as a header for the person. State the scenario (his home tool) in prose. If a re-capture is not wanted, add one sentence that names all the edits, including ticket 2's brand. It should also say the result is credited to the ticket text alone.

**M4. Lines 418-426: the chapter never models a no-AI decision, and its own fix rule points the other way.**
The rigor step asks for "no AI here" as a real decision. The chapter gives three one-line tests and then defers to the lab (line 444, "Decided in Skills Lab 11A"). Example: a plumbing contractor's office deciding a warranty claim from a customer's photos and payment record. Run the three tests in a table and fill the Decision and Why fields.
