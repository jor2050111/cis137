# CIS137: Artificial Intelligence Fundamentals

Welcome to the CIS137 textbook. Learn how AI models work, direct them with prompts, audit what they produce, and select and defend AI solutions as the human in the loop.

## About This Textbook

This textbook is written for **students in Phoenix College's bachelor's pathway in information technology** and for anyone taking a first AI course. It has no prerequisites. Every chapter teaches from zero, and no programming is assumed. You use AI tools from the first chapter on, so plan to create free accounts on more than one tool. Your syllabus names the current roster, and each chapter says which kind of tool it needs.

CIS137 is the first course in the Foundations of Applied Artificial Intelligence certificate (CIS137, CIS144, CIS218). This book covers CIS137. It points ahead to the next two courses without teaching them.

### Course Learning Outcomes

This textbook teaches to the official MCCCD course competencies for CIS137. Each one carries its Revised Bloom's Taxonomy level:

* **CLO 1 (Evaluate):** Evaluate AI-generated responses for accuracy.
* **CLO 2 (Analyze):** Differentiate the major types of Artificial Intelligence (AI).
* **CLO 3 (Apply):** Demonstrate effective prompting strategies to improve AI outputs.
* **CLO 4 (Analyze):** Analyze the current state of AI and its impact on society, including strengths and weaknesses.
* **CLO 5 (Apply):** Apply ethical guidelines to make responsible decisions when using AI tools.
* **CLO 6 (Evaluate):** Select appropriate AI solutions to address specific problems.

## How This Textbook Is Organized

The textbook has **4 parts** and **12 chapters**. The chapters are schedule-neutral. An instructor can map them into a 9-, 12-, 14-, or 16-week course without changing the content.

### Part I: Foundations of AI and Generative Tools (Chapters 1-3)

Learn what AI is, how the tools make their output, and where they fail. By the end of Part I you can tell the types of AI apart, hold a useful conversation with a chat assistant, and check what it tells you.

### Part II: Prompt Engineering (Chapters 4-6)

Build prompts from their parts. Apply the strategies that raise output quality. Refine your prompts in loops. You finish this part with a prompt library you can reuse.

### Part III: AI Regulation and Ethics (Chapters 7-9)

Audit AI output for accuracy and bias. Read the laws and frameworks that govern AI use. Weigh what AI does for a business and for society.

### Part IV: Selecting and Applying AI Tools (Chapters 10-12)

Survey and compare AI platforms. Match tools to scenarios. Finish with a project: an AI solution for a scenario, a presentation, and a reflection on the ethics.

## Technology Stack

| Tool | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Chat assistants** | Draft, summarize, brainstorm, and answer questions in conversation |
| **Research tools** | Find and cite sources, then check AI claims against them |
| **Image generators** | Create and edit images from text prompts |
| **AI inside everyday apps** | Recognize the assistant already built into your mail, documents, and phone |
| **A text editor or word processor** | Record prompts, outputs, and your evaluations for every lab |

The tools and their account steps live in callouts inside the chapters, because tool names change faster than the skills they teach.
