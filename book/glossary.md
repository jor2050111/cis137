# CIS137 Glossary

This glossary defines every technical term used in the CIS137 textbook. Each chapter bolds a term at first use, and the Key Terms list at the end of each chapter points you here for review. Every chapter uses these exact definitions. Vendor and product names do not appear here, because the skills outlast any one tool.

## A

AI detector
:   A tool that scores a piece of text on how likely it is to be AI-generated. Published studies have found these tools inaccurate, and biased against people writing in a second language, so a score is never proof.

AI winter
:   A period when funding and interest in AI collapsed after promised results failed to arrive. The field saw two, in the 1970s and around 1990. The pattern (breakthrough, promise, lag, collapse) is a tool for judging today's claims.

AI-generated content
:   Any text, image, audio, or video that an AI tool produced, in whole or in part. You usually cannot identify it by looking, so evidence of process and disclosure matter more than style.

artificial general intelligence (AGI)
:   A hypothetical system that could match human ability across any task it was handed, including tasks it was never trained for. No system meets that bar today. Treat "general" in a product claim as a claim to test.

artificial intelligence (AI)
:   A family of techniques for getting computers to do tasks that normally need human judgment, such as recognizing a face, sorting mail, writing a paragraph, or choosing a move. AI is not one technology. The types differ in where their behavior comes from.

attribution
:   Giving credit to the people and sources whose work you used, and checking that each source says what you claim it says.

## C

chat assistant
:   A generative AI tool you talk to in plain language. It drafts, summarizes, explains, brainstorms, and answers questions. The main tool of this course.

citation
:   A reference that names a source well enough for someone else to find it: the publisher, the title, the date, and the location.

claim
:   A single statement in a response that can be checked against a source and found true or false. Names, numbers, dates, citations, and statements about change or cause are the claims to check first.

consent
:   A person's clear permission to use their words, image, voice, or story for a specific purpose.

content credentials
:   A record embedded in a file that says how it was made and edited, including whether an AI tool produced it. A missing credential proves nothing, because credentials can be stripped when a file is copied.

content ownership
:   The question of who holds the rights to a piece of work, including work made with AI help.

context window
:   The amount of text, counted in tokens, that a model can consider at one time, including your messages, its replies, and any pasted document. Text outside the window does not exist for the model, which is why a new conversation starts with no memory.

custom instructions
:   Standing text you save in a tool's settings that the tool adds to every conversation you start, or to every conversation in one workspace. The rule about what never goes into an AI tool applies to it, because it is stored with your account.

## D

decomposition
:   Splitting a job with several stages into separate prompts, one stage each, and checking each result before you use it in the next stage. It puts a checkpoint where an error would start.

deep learning
:   Machine learning that uses a neural network with many layers. Deep learning made computers good at seeing and hearing, and it is the foundation of generative AI.

deepfake
:   Synthetic audio, video, or an image that makes a real person appear to say or do something they never did.

digital object identifier (DOI)
:   A permanent identifier that publishers assign to a scholarly article, written as a link that starts with doi.org. A DOI a model writes from memory can be wrong even when the article is real, so open it before you cite it.

disclosure
:   A plain statement of whether and how AI helped make something. A disclosure statement says what the AI did, what you did, and what you checked, and adds nothing else.

## E

evaluation criteria
:   The checkable tests you write before the first run of a prompt to decide whether an output does its job, such as a word limit, a fact that must appear, or nothing added. Each one passes or fails on evidence you can point to.

expert system
:   A rule-based system from the 1970s and 1980s that captured a specialist's knowledge as hundreds or thousands of if-then rules. Expensive to maintain and brittle outside its narrow domain.

## F

few-shot prompting
:   Including a few finished examples of the output you want in the prompt, followed by the new case. The examples teach shape and voice, and the model also copies habits you did not mean to teach. Also called an example-led prompt.

fine-tuning
:   An extra round of training on a narrower set of examples after a model's first training. Fine-tuning on sample conversations and people's ratings turns a text-predicting model into a chat assistant, but it does not give the model a way to check facts.

five parts of a prompt
:   Role, Task, Context, Format, and Constraints: the five things a prompt can tell a model, each answering a question the model would otherwise answer with a guess. Role says whose knowledge and voice to borrow, Task names the one action and deliverable, Context supplies the facts it cannot guess, Format sets the shape of the output, and Constraints set the limits on content and behavior.

free tier
:   The version of a tool you can use at no cost, with limits such as a daily message cap, a smaller or slower model, or fewer features. Every lab in this course fits inside a free tier.

## G

generating era
:   The era of AI that began in 2017 with the transformer and reached the public in 2022. Its models produce new text, images, and audio from a prompt. It overlaps the earlier eras instead of replacing them.

generative AI
:   AI that produces new content, such as text, images, audio, or video, in response to a prompt. A generative model is a machine learning model whose job is to make new content, not to sort existing content.

good enough
:   The point where a prompt meets every must-pass criterion in a fresh run and a re-run, and another version is unlikely to repay the time it costs. Past that point, a quick human edit beats another version.

grounding
:   Supplying the source an answer must come from, telling the model to answer only from it, and saying what to do when the source is silent. It turns "is this true?" into "is this in the source?", which you can check.

guardrail
:   A negative constraint in a prompt aimed at a specific harm you can name in advance, such as a price, a promise, medical or legal advice, or a client's name.

## H

hallucination
:   A response, or part of one, that is fluent and confident but false or unsupported, such as an invented fact, quote, or citation. It follows from how a model works: it predicts likely text, and a likely-looking detail is not always a true one.

human in the loop
:   The person who directs an AI tool, checks its work, and stays responsible for what goes out under their name, including the decision not to use AI at all.

## I

image generator
:   A generative AI tool that produces a picture from a text prompt. Most also edit a picture you upload.

## K

knowledge cutoff
:   The date after which a model's training data ends. The model knows nothing about later events unless the tool searches for them, and it may present outdated information as current.

## L

large language model (LLM)
:   A deep learning model trained on enormous amounts of text to predict which words come next. Large language models are the models behind chat assistants.

lead
:   A pointer to where an answer might be found, such as an organization or a search term, not the answer itself. A lead becomes a fact only when you open the source and confirm it.

learning era
:   The era of AI, from the 2000s and accelerating in 2012, in which behavior is learned from data instead of written as rules. Learning ideas date to the 1950s. Data and hardware made them win.

## M

machine learning
:   A type of AI that is given examples instead of rules and works out the patterns on its own. The examples are its training data, and the learned patterns form its model.

model
:   The set of patterns a machine learning system learned from its training data, which it uses to decide about something it has never seen before.

multimodal
:   Able to handle more than one kind of input or output, such as text, images, and audio, in one tool or one conversation.

## N

narrow AI
:   AI built for one job or a cluster of related jobs. It cannot take on a job it was not built or trained for, plan reliably on its own, or check its own facts. Every AI tool in use today is narrow in this sense, including chat assistants.

neural network
:   A model built from layers of simple connected units, each passing a signal to the next. A network with many layers is deep, which is where deep learning gets its name.

## O

Output Checklist
:   Five checks you run on every response before you use it: did it do the task, right shape, every claim traceable, nothing added, and tone fits the audience. Each check gets a Yes, Partly, or No and the quoted line that decided it.

## P

persona
:   A role for the model described in enough detail, such as expertise, experience, and point of view, that it shapes what the response includes and how it sounds.

primary source
:   The organization or document where a fact starts, such as the agency that collected the data, the study that ran the test, or the text of a rule.

privacy check
:   A short list, made before you send a prompt, of what goes into an AI tool and what stays out.

prompt
:   The request you give a generative AI tool: a question, an instruction, or a description of what you want it to produce.

prompt card
:   A one-page record of a reusable prompt in standard fields: name, job, the full prompt with its parts labeled, an example input, what good output looks like, the strategies used, the last run, and notes. Slots stand in for private details, which go in only at send time.

prompt library
:   A shared set of prompt cards that a team keeps, runs, and improves, so a good prompt belongs to the team instead of one person's chat history.

prompt log
:   A plain text or Markdown file where you record each prompt you send, the tool, the date, the response you received (copied exactly), and your notes on it. The evidence every Skills Lab in this course is built from.

Prompt Strategy Checklist
:   Nine named prompting strategies, in order, each with a test you run on a prompt: specificity and context, examples (few-shot), output structure, step-by-step reasoning request, decomposition, persona and audience framing, grounding, clarifying questions first, and negative constraints and guardrails.

prompt type
:   The form a request takes, which tells the model what kind of response you want. The five types are instruction, question, completion, role play, and example-led (few-shot), and you choose among them by the job.

## R

refinement log
:   A record of a prompt's versions, one row per version, with the one change, the score against the criteria, and the decision to keep or revert. It shows a colleague what was tried and why.

refinement loop
:   The cycle for improving a prompt: write the criteria, run, score, diagnose, change one thing, run again, and stop when the criteria are met.

reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)
:   A training method in which people compare a model's answers and pick the better one, and the model is then trained toward the answers people prefer. It makes assistants more helpful and polite, and research links it to sycophancy.

research tool
:   A generative AI tool that answers a question with citations to sources you can open and check. It combines web search with generation.

rule-based system
:   A system that follows instructions a person wrote out in advance: if this, then that. Predictable and explainable, but it fails on any case the rule writer did not foresee. This book counts it as AI when its rules stand in for a judgment a person would otherwise make.

rules era
:   The first era of AI, from the 1950s into the 1990s, in which a program's behavior was written by hand as rules. Its high point was Deep Blue in 1997.

## S

strategy
:   A named move you add to a prompt to raise the quality of what comes back, with a test you can run on the prompt before you send it.

sycophancy
:   A model's tendency to agree with the person it is talking to, even when that person is wrong, such as changing a correct answer under pushback or praising flawed work. Because of it, asking a model whether it is sure is not a check.

system prompt
:   Instructions that a tool or app sends to the model before your first message, usually out of your sight, to set the assistant's role and rules. Standing instructions you type at the start of a conversation work like a system prompt for that conversation, though the model still reads them as your message, and tools give their own system prompt more weight.

## T

token
:   The unit of text a model reads and writes: a whole word, a piece of a word, a number, or a punctuation mark. Tool limits and context windows are counted in tokens.

training data
:   The examples a machine learning system learns from. The model inherits whatever the training data contains, including its mistakes and its bias.

Turing test
:   A test Alan Turing proposed in 1950 as an imitation game: a machine passes if a person conversing with it by text cannot reliably tell it from a human.

## V

version
:   A numbered, saved copy of a prompt that differs from the one before it by a recorded change. Versions let you go back to the last prompt that worked.

## Z

zero-shot prompting
:   Asking a model for an output with no examples of that output in the prompt.
