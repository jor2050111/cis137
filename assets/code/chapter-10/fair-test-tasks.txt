Copperwind Fair Test: Three Help Desk Tasks
===========================================

From: Mei Lin
To: the AI adoption assistant (you)
Subject: The three tasks for the Comparison Matrix

The pilot ends this quarter, and I have to tell the owner which tools
Copperwind should use next year and for which jobs. Before anyone
argues about favorites, I want every candidate tool tested on the same
three Deskside tasks, with the same inputs, scored the same way. Use
the inputs below exactly as written. Everything in this file is
fictional and carries no client names or other identifiers, so you may
paste it into a tool once your privacy plan allows it.

For each task you get the input and the checks a good result must
pass. Those checks are the same for every tool you test.


Task 1: A first reply to a printer-offline ticket
-------------------------------------------------

The ticket, word for word:

  "The printer by the front window shows offline on my computer, but
  the person at the next desk can still print to it. I need to print
  shipping labels before the 2 p.m. pickup."

Deskside steps for a printer that shows offline, in this order:

  (1) Check that the printer's network cable is plugged in, or that
      its screen shows a Wi-Fi connection.
  (2) On one office computer, open the printer list, remove the
      printer, and add it again.
  (3) If it still shows offline, reply to this ticket, and a
      technician will connect remotely during business hours.

A good reply:

  * uses only the three Deskside steps, in order, and adds no other
    step
  * promises no time (not even the 2 p.m. pickup)
  * runs under 100 words
  * is signed "Deskside Support"


Task 2: A four-bullet summary of a short client update
------------------------------------------------------

The client update, word for word (the client's name is removed):

  "Hi Deskside team. Six of our staff get new laptops in December.
  The laptops arrive at our office on Wednesday, December 2. We would
  like Copperwind to set them up on Thursday, December 3, and Friday,
  December 4, three each day, so nobody loses a full workday. The old
  laptops go back to the leasing company, which picks them up on
  Monday, December 7, so please copy each person's files to the new
  laptop before then. Our budget for the setup work is $900. Call me
  before you go over it. One caution: two of the six people work from
  home on Fridays, so please schedule them for Thursday. Thanks, the
  office manager"

A good summary:

  * has exactly four bullets
  * matches every date, count, and dollar amount in the update
  * keeps the Friday caution
  * adds nothing the update does not say


Task 3: A plain-language explanation of two-step sign-in
--------------------------------------------------------

The audience is the office staff at one client company. Most of them
are not technical. The facts, word for word:

  Starting Monday, December 7, 2026, every staff email account uses
  two-step sign-in. After you type your password, you approve the
  sign-in in the authenticator app on your work phone, or you type the
  six-digit code the app shows. We do not use text-message codes. When
  you set it up, you get ten backup codes. Keep them somewhere safe,
  not in your email. If you lose your phone, call Deskside during
  support hours, Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m.

Our standing promise, word for word:

  "No one from Copperwind will ever ask you for your password or for
  a sign-in code."

A good explanation:

  * runs under 150 words
  * uses plain words, and explains any technical word in the same
    sentence
  * matches every fact above and adds no other method of signing in
  * includes the standing promise word for word
