Capstone Brief: Your Own IT Project
===================================

This option carries the same weight and the same artifacts as the
Copperwind capstone. You bring the problem. Your instructor approves
it before you start Part 2 of the lab.


What qualifies
--------------

A project qualifies when all four of these hold:

  1. It is a job you are doing now: a home lab, a club project, a
     course project in another class, or a task at work or in a
     volunteer role.
  2. A person other than you will act on the result. That person is
     your stakeholder: a supervisor, a club officer, a project
     teammate, a client of your volunteer work, or your instructor if
     no one else fits.
  3. The deliverable is text a person can check: a runbook, a test
     plan, a build checklist, configuration notes, a migration plan,
     a summary, or a set of instructions.
  4. At least two parts of the job can go to an AI tool through a Work
     Order, and at least one part should stay with a person.

Examples that qualify: a runbook for a quarterly password rotation, a
test plan for a VPN change, a build checklist for a home lab server,
configuration notes for a small office network, a migration plan for a
ticket system.

If your project needs code or configuration syntax, keep the Work
Order's deliverable a plan, a checklist, or notes. Reviewing code an
AI tool writes is the work of CIS144 and CIS218, so this project does
not ask for it.


The privacy rule
----------------

  * Nothing identifying goes into a tool: no names, addresses, account
    numbers, IP addresses, serial numbers, or passwords from a real
    person or a real network. Replace them with labels before you
    paste anything (Server-1, User-A, 10.0.0.x).
  * Nothing you do not have the right to share. Work files, client
    files, and a club's member list belong to someone else. Ask the
    owner first, and write down who said yes and when.
  * If your workplace or school has its own AI rules, they come first.
    Name them in your Ethics Statement.
  * If the organization has no AI rules, write the ones your project
    follows, using the AI Use Policy template in your toolkit-starter
    folder.


The same artifacts as the Copperwind option
-------------------------------------------

  * A Requirements Sheet, written before you name a tool.
  * A Tool Survey card for each tool you consider.
  * At least two Work Orders, each with its runs and a scored result.
  * A Verification Trail with its README.
  * A Presentation outline, and the presentation itself in the format
    your instructor sets, given to your stakeholder or to someone
    standing in for them.
  * An Ethics Statement.
  * Your Toolkit, with its README.


Before you start Part 2
-----------------------

Send your instructor three lines: the job, the stakeholder (by role),
and what will never go into a tool. Start Part 2 once your instructor
approves.
