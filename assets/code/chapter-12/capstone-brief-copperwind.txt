Copperwind Capstone Brief: The Year-Ahead Recommendation
========================================================

From: Mei Lin, AI pilot lead
To: the AI adoption assistant (you)
Subject: What we adopt for the next year

The pilot ends this quarter. Before it does, I have to tell the owner
what Copperwind adopts for the next year: which jobs get an AI tool,
which tools, under what rules, and how we pilot whatever is new. The
owner approves spending. The operations manager signs the AI Use
Policy. I carry the recommendation to the owner, so you present it to
me first. If I cannot defend a line of it, it does not go to the owner.

This brief gives you everything you need. You do not need any file
from an earlier project.


What the recommendation must contain
------------------------------------

  1. The jobs. For each job you looked at, one of four decisions:
     adopt, extend the pilot, decline, or no AI here. At least one job
     must be "no AI here," with the reason.
  2. The tools. For each job that gets a tool, which type of tool and
     why, from your Tool Survey cards. Name a tool only if you tested
     it yourself.
  3. The rules. Which sections of the AI Use Policy each job touches,
     by section name, and anything the policy must add first.
  4. The pilot plan. For each "extend the pilot" job: what you will
     measure, for how long, who checks the drafts, and what result
     would change the decision.
  5. What changes for the technicians. Nobody is being replaced, and
     Copperwind hires one more Deskside technician this quarter either
     way. Say what changes in their day.


Facts you can rely on
---------------------

  * Copperwind IT Services is a managed IT services provider in
    Phoenix, Arizona, with about 30 employees and roughly 40 client
    companies. All our clients are in Arizona. Some are medical and
    physical therapy offices, whose tickets can name a patient.
  * Deskside has 5 technicians. Network Ops has 3.
  * Deskside closes about 160 tickets a week.
  * Support hours are Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. Our target is
    a first reply within 30 minutes during support hours.
  * Deskside sends the first reply on a remote VPN ticket, and Network
    Ops fixes the connection.
  * Copperwind has no client portal.
  * Clients reach Deskside by opening a ticket or by calling the
    Deskside line at 602-555-0149.
  * The first-reply pilot (two weeks): 40 tickets had an AI-drafted
    first reply. 31 were sent with light edits, 7 were rewritten, and
    2 were caught with an invented step before sending (a VPN
    reinstall, and a self-service portal we do not have). A technician
    takes about 6 minutes to draft a first reply by hand and about 2
    minutes to send a light-edit draft. The pilot did not time the 7
    rewrites.
  * The vendor's quote for the pilot tool is $30 per user per month.
  * Our AI Use Policy has seven sections: Purpose, Approved tools,
    What never goes in, Review before use, Disclosure, Who to ask,
    Review date.


Jobs to consider
----------------

Run at least two of these through a Work Order. You may add a job of
your own from Deskside's work if you tell me why.

These are the same three Deskside tasks as the fair test, with the same
inputs and checks, copied from it word for word.


Job A: A first reply to a printer-offline ticket
------------------------------------------------

The ticket, word for word:

  "The printer by the front window shows offline on my computer, but
  the person at the next desk can still print to it. I need to print
  shipping labels before the 2 p.m. pickup."

Deskside steps for a printer that shows offline, in this order:

  (1) Check that the printer's network cable is plugged in, or that
      its screen shows a Wi-Fi connection.
  (2) On one office computer, open the printer list, remove the
      printer, and add it again.
  (3) If it still shows offline, reply to this ticket, and a
      technician will connect remotely during business hours.

A good reply:

  * uses only the three Deskside steps, in order, and adds no other
    step
  * promises no time (not even the 2 p.m. pickup)
  * runs under 100 words
  * is signed "Deskside Support"


Job B: A four-bullet summary of a short client update
-----------------------------------------------------

The client update, word for word (the client's name is removed):

  "Hi Deskside team. Six of our staff get new laptops in December.
  The laptops arrive at our office on Wednesday, December 2. We would
  like Copperwind to set them up on Thursday, December 3, and Friday,
  December 4, three each day, so nobody loses a full workday. The old
  laptops go back to the leasing company, which picks them up on
  Monday, December 7, so please copy each person's files to the new
  laptop before then. Our budget for the setup work is $900. Call me
  before you go over it. One caution: two of the six people work from
  home on Fridays, so please schedule them for Thursday. Thanks, the
  office manager"

A good summary:

  * has exactly four bullets
  * matches every date, count, and dollar amount in the update
  * keeps the Friday caution
  * adds nothing the update does not say


Job C: A plain-language explanation of two-step sign-in
-------------------------------------------------------

The audience is the office staff at one client company. Most of them
are not technical. The facts, word for word:

  Starting Monday, December 7, 2026, every staff email account uses
  two-step sign-in. After you type your password, you approve the
  sign-in in the authenticator app on your work phone, or you type the
  six-digit code the app shows. We do not use text-message codes. When
  you set it up, you get ten backup codes. Keep them somewhere safe,
  not in your email. If you lose your phone, call Deskside during
  support hours, Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m.

Our standing promise, word for word:

  "No one from Copperwind will ever ask you for your password or for
  a sign-in code."

A good explanation:

  * runs under 150 words
  * uses plain words, and explains any technical word in the same
    sentence
  * matches every fact above and adds no other method of signing in
  * includes the standing promise word for word

What never goes into a prompt
-----------------------------

The rule is the one you have used all course: nothing identifying goes
into a tool, and nothing you do not have the right to share. For this
brief, that means:

  * Client names, requester names, account details, and anything from
    a medical or physical therapy client's ticket that could name a
    patient.
  * Payment details of any kind.
  * My private cell number. You do not need it for any of this work.

Everything else in this brief is fictional and may go into a tool.
